Decision Summary

"The aerospace and automotive supply chain lesson: the cost of a supplier failure compounds daily. The only lever is early detection — but only if you can distinguish theoretical nameplate capacity from executable capacity. Suppliers that couldn't prove executable capacity (machine run-rates + shift data) were disqualified from winning next program expansion. That alignment of supply risk consequence with business outcome is what turned a monitoring program into a strategic capability."

Detection Economics

8 days faster detection × $300K–$500K daily exposure for a critical engine line = $2.4M–$4.0M protected per recovery event. At 8–12 recovery events per year, this compounds to $19M–$48M in risk coverage — before accounting for the 3–4 month earlier intervention window that prevented constraints from becoming production stops entirely. The framework's ROI was not in the detection tool — it was in the credibility of capacity data (executable vs. nameplate) and the pre-agreed escalation process that removed weeks of social friction from the critical path.
